Privacy

CyPress has no accounts and no ads, and it doesn’t sell or share anything about you. Your feeds, settings and reading history stay on your own device. Here is the full picture, including the places where your device talks to other services.

Last updated October 2026.

What stays on your device

  • The sites you follow, your settings, saved stories, highlights, notes and reading history.
  • Saved copies of stories and, if you turn it on, their photos, so you can read offline.
  • The app has no analytics and no tracking of what you read.

What leaves your device, and why

To show you news, CyPress has to ask other services for it. These requests carry the address of the page being loaded, and, like any web request, your IP address. They don’t carry your name, because CyPress doesn’t have one for you.

  • The news sites themselves. The Android app loads feeds, stories and photos straight from each site.
  • Relay services (web version). A web page isn’t allowed to read most sites directly, so the web version sends the request through a relay. It tries CyPress’s own relay on Cloudflare first. If that fails, it can fall back to free public relays (allorigins.win, codetabs.com and corsproxy.io). Each relay can see which feed or article address was requested. In Settings you can point the web version at a relay you run yourself.
  • Getting a full story. When a feed only has a short excerpt, CyPress may try other routes for the full text: the Jina reader (r.jina.ai), the Internet Archive (web.archive.org), Morss.it (morss.it), and Google News search to find the same story elsewhere. Each of these sees the address of that story, or of its feed.
  • Site icons. Small site icons come from Google’s icon service, which sees the site’s domain name.
  • Fonts. Some themes load fonts from Google Fonts, and the dyslexia-friendly font comes from jsDelivr. These services see your IP address when a font loads.
  • Updates (Android). About once a day the Android app asks GitHub for the newest release. If you accept an update, the app downloads it from GitHub and asks Android to install it. CyPress isn’t on the Play Store, so this is how updates reach you.
  • Location (optional). The “Near you” feature and the weather and sunset features use your location only after you tap them and Android asks you. Your coordinates go to OpenStreetMap’s Nominatim service to find a place name, and to Open-Meteo for the weather. Nothing is stored on a CyPress server.
  • Sharing a story. When you share a story, its title and a short excerpt travel inside the link. If you create a short link, that text is stored on CyPress’s Cloudflare account for about 13 months so the link keeps working.

This website

cypressreader.com is served through Cloudflare, which can count page views with its cookie-free Web Analytics. CyPress doesn’t add ads, tracking cookies or third-party trackers of its own. The home page loads its fonts from Google Fonts.

Your choices

  • Turn on Offline mode or Only on Wi-Fi in Settings to limit what the app downloads.
  • Use the Android app to skip the relay services, or run your own relay for the web version.
  • Don’t use “Near you”, the weather features or the “Keep photos for offline” setting, and none of those requests happen.
  • Delete everything CyPress keeps with Erase everything in Settings, or by clearing the app’s storage in Android.
