Privacy

CyPress Reader doesn’t need an account, and it doesn’t sell or share anything about you. Your feeds, settings and reading history stay on your own device. Here is the full picture, including the places where your device talks to other services.

Last updated October 14, 2026.

What stays on your device

  • The sites you follow, your settings, saved stories, highlights, notes and reading history.
  • Saved copies of stories and, if you turn it on, their photos, so you can read offline.
  • The app has no analytics and no tracking of what you read.

What leaves your device, and why

To show you news, CyPress Reader has to ask other services for it. These requests carry the address of the page being loaded, and, like any web request, your IP address. They don’t carry your name, because CyPress Reader doesn’t have one for you.

  • The news sites themselves. The Android app loads feeds, stories and photos straight from each site.
  • Relay services (web version). A web page isn’t allowed to read most sites directly, so the web version sends the request through a relay. It tries CyPress Reader’s own relay on Cloudflare first. If that fails, it can fall back to public relays (allorigins.win, codetabs.com and corsproxy.io). Each relay can see which feed or article address was requested. In Settings you can point the web version at a relay you run yourself.
  • A last-resort feed reader. If a feed can’t be read any other way, CyPress Reader may ask rss2json.com (api.rss2json.com), a service that reads a feed and sends it back as plain data. It sees the address of that feed and your IP address.
  • Getting a full story. When a feed only has a short excerpt, CyPress Reader may try other routes for the full text: the Jina reader (r.jina.ai), Morss.it (morss.it), and Google News search to find the same story elsewhere. Each of these sees the address of that story, or of its feed. CyPress Reader does not try to get around a paywall: a story a site keeps for subscribers is shown as the site gives it.
  • Videos (YouTube). A story with a video shows its thumbnail from YouTube (i.ytimg.com). Only when you tap play does the app load YouTube’s privacy-enhanced player (youtube-nocookie.com), which then sees your IP address. Nothing is loaded from the player before you tap.
  • Translate story (optional). In the Android app the story is translated on the phone itself, and its text is never sent anywhere. The first time you translate from or into a language, the app downloads that language’s small translation pack (about 30 MB) from Google; only the download request is made, never the story. If your phone can’t translate on the device, CyPress Reader asks first, and only when you choose Open does Google Translate (translate.google.com) load, which then sees the story’s address and your IP address. Translated headlines (a per-site or all-sites option in the Android app) work the same way: the headlines are translated on the phone and only the language-pack download is made. Google’s translation component can also send Google anonymous technical details about the feature (such as that a translation ran and whether it failed), never the story or its headlines.
  • Site icons. Small site icons come from Google’s icon service, which sees the site’s domain name.
  • Fonts in the app. The app’s fonts are built in. Only if you choose one of the extra reading fonts in settings is that font loaded from Google Fonts, and the dyslexia-friendly font comes from jsDelivr. These services see your IP address when a font loads.
  • Updates (Android). About once a day the Android app asks GitHub for the newest release. If you accept an update, the app downloads it from GitHub and asks Android to install it. CyPress Reader isn’t on the Play Store, so this is how updates reach you.
  • Local news (optional). CyPress Reader never asks for your location. With “Near you”, the place name you type (for example a town or city) is sent to Google News and Bing News as a search, and their results become feeds. Those services also see your IP address. Places you save stay on your device only.
  • Finding new feeds (optional). When you type a topic or feeling in Add a feed and the built-in list has little to show for it (or you tap “Search the web”), the words you typed are sent to Feedly’s public search (cloud.feedly.com), which sees them along with your IP address. Addresses and the names of sites in the built-in list are not sent. The feeds it lists are then fetched from their own sites to measure how active they are.
  • Sharing a story. When you share a story, its headline, source name, web address, color and date (and its picture address) are packed into a CyPress Reader link. With “Shorten shared links” on (Settings, Backup, Sync and About), only when you tap Share, that link is sent to CyPress Reader’s own link service on Cloudflare, which stores it for about 13 months so the short link cypressreader.com/s/… keeps working; Cloudflare sees your device’s address as any website does. If you turn the setting off, or the service does not answer, CyPress Reader shares the story’s own web address instead and contacts no one. When someone opens a short link in the Android app, the app asks the same link service for the story behind that code; a long link is read on the phone.

This website

cypressreader.com uses no analytics at all: no page-view counting, no cookies, no trackers. The home page makes no third-party requests; its fonts are hosted on the site itself. The site is served through Cloudflare, which, like any web host, sees each request (your IP address and the page asked for) and keeps standard server logs. We do not add Cloudflare’s analytics script, or any other script that counts visits, to our pages.

Your choices

  • Turn on Offline mode or Only on Wi-Fi in Settings to limit what the app downloads.
  • Use the Android app to skip the relay services, or run your own relay for the web version.
  • Don’t use “Near you” or the “Keep photos for offline” setting, and none of those requests happen.
  • Delete everything CyPress Reader keeps with Erase everything in Settings, or by clearing the app’s storage in Android.

The demo on the home page

The “Try it live” demo on cypressreader.com runs a copy of CyPress Reader with made-up stories. It keeps everything in memory, sends nothing to other services, and never touches the sites, settings or saved stories in your own CyPress Reader.
