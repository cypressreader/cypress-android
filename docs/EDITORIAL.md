# World Edition — Editorial Playbook (for Claude)

This is the standing editorial reference for the World Edition. Read it before building or changing any World Edition surface. It is the magazine's house style. When a rule here conflicts with a generic UI habit, this file wins.

## Vocabulary
- **Masthead:** "CyPress" + "The World Edition." On the cover and back cover only.
- **Cover lines:** short teasers on the cover for stories inside (not the lead). A cover has cover lines; a story opener does not. This is what makes a cover a cover.
- **Kicker:** a short label above a headline (e.g., "TODAY'S LEAD," a section name). A real editorial label, never a stat ("8 of 25 outlets" is data, not a kicker).
- **Stand-first / deck:** the italic summary under a headline.
- **Byline:** outlet + date (+ credit where there's a photo).
- **Pull quote:** a large, set-apart line from the story, with "FROM THE STORY · [outlet]" beneath it. One per story where the story has a quotable line.
- **Folio / running head:** the small line at the top of every page — section, page number, "CyPress · The World Edition." Quiet, consistent, never overlapping body text.
- **End mark:** a small red square + "END OF THE STORY" at the end of every story. Every story ends; every ending is marked.
- **Section opener:** the first page of a section — big section number + section name + lead headline + deck + byline + a photo (or a distinct drawn mark). A section opener starts on a fresh spread.
- **Spread:** the unit of design. Never design a single page; always design the spread as a whole.

## Principles
1. **Design the spread as a whole.** Balance content evenly across the two pages. Never leave half a spread blank.
2. **Hierarchy.** The most important thing is the loudest. The cover's masthead + main image are loudest; a story's headline is next; a section number/kicker is quiet. A label must never outshout the content it labels.
3. **The three engagement features are layout, main image, headline.** Every spread needs all three working.
4. **Most visible are the outer, upper parts of a spread.** Put the strongest element there — the cover's main image, a story opener's headline.
5. **Line length 50–70 characters.** Shorter lines break too often; longer lines lose the reader.
6. **Break up big copy blocks.** Pull quotes, highlighted blocks, subheads. No wall of text.
7. **Entry points.** Drop caps and pull quotes give the reader a place to start. A drop cap must clear the text — it never overlaps a word.
8. **Viewing direction is top-left to bottom-right.** Don't overcomplicate the flow.
9. **White space is a design element.** A pause (a section break, an ending) is created by space and a turn, not by cramming the next thing onto the same spread.
10. **The cover sells the issue.** Masthead + main image + cover lines. If it could be mistaken for a story opener, it's not a cover yet.

## Hard rules (never break these)
- **No overlapping type.** No drop cap, kicker, section number, rule, or label may overlap a word of body text, a headline, or another label. Check every page at phone width and Fold width for collisions, descender/ascender clipping, and stacked-type spacing.
- **No hyphenated headlines.** Headlines use whole words only.
- **No repeated image.** Never use the same drawn image or photo twice in one edition. Every photo-less story gets its own distinct drawn mark.
- **No story that opens on the publisher's site.** If a story can't be fetched and cleaned, swap it for the next-best story from the same section that loads (verified ready before publishing). A shelf may run short but never carries a story that opens on the publisher's site. The ready line must read 18/18 (or honestly short).
- **Every story has an end mark.** Every section opener starts on a fresh spread. A story ends its spread; the next section starts after a turn.
- **Every image has a caption + credit.** A photo credit is "Photo: [outlet]"; a drawn image is "Drawn for this edition."
- **The cover has cover lines.** A story opener does not. The cover's main image is dominant (full-bleed or the largest element on the cover).
- **The turn is a page curl** (like Apple Books / Google Play Books), fast, and it must not freeze or show a duplicated page. Prerender only the two pages involved.

## What to steal from each reference magazine
- **Influencia:** illustration as the focal point (cover direction).
- **Lucky Peach:** photo + illustrative annotation (cover direction).
- **Bloomberg Businessweek:** understated quirks, infographics (use outlet counts as a graphic element, not a kicker).
- **Port:** restrained classic (the fallback when in doubt).
- **Sanahunt Times:** enlarged type + dramatic photo (section-opener direction).
- **Esquire:** covers crowded with cover lines around an image-centric focal point (the cover-lines model).
- **iD:** bold type that lets the photography shine (let the opener's photo be prominent, not a strip).

## Verification (do this before reporting done)
- Render every spread at Fold width (884×1104) and phone width (412×915). Look at the pictures.
- Check for: text overlaps/collisions, descender/ascender clipping, spacing between stacked type, truncation, alignment, repeated images, hyphenated headlines.
- Confirm the ready line reads 18/18 (or honestly short) and every story opens in the app.
- Confirm the turn is a page curl, fast, with no duplicated page and no freeze — watch it move.
- Confirm the cover has cover lines and is unmistakably a cover.
- Confirm every section opener starts on a fresh spread.
