Your privacy

Stays on this device

Your feeds, folders, saved stories, highlights, reading history and settings. There is no CyPress account and no tracking or analytics.

What leaves the device

Fetching feeds and stories: the Android app contacts each site you follow directly. The web version goes through a relay (CyPress’s own, or free public ones) because browsers can’t read most sites directly. When a story is cut short, CyPress may try the Jina reader or Morss.it to get the rest. It never tries to get past a paywall. They see that story’s address. The full list is at cypressreader.com/privacy.html.

Site icons come from Google’s favicon service, which sees the site name, not your reading.

If a site blocks feed readers, CyPress asks Bing News or Google News for its headlines. Those services see which site you asked about.

Updates check GitHub for a new version.

Sync, only if you turn it on, uses your own relay. Everything is locked with your sync code before it leaves.

    Relay setup

    CyPress has no account and no server of ours, so your reading stays under your control. A relay is a tiny free service that only you own. It lets your devices sync, and it fetches feeds and articles for sites that block feed readers, including TechCrunch. It takes about five minutes to set up, and you can skip it unless you want sync.

    1. Use the code above (it now includes sync). If you set up your relay before, paste it again and deploy.
    2. In Cloudflare, open Storage & Databases, then KV, and create a namespace named cypress-sync.
    3. Open your worker, go to Settings, then Bindings, add a KV namespace binding with the variable name SYNC, pick cypress-sync, and deploy.
